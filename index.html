Unit 1: The Most Interesting Lenses To Me

  • This first unit was entirely about learning how to analyze texts in a more rigorous manner than we previously had
    • This unit encourages analyzing text through a certain fixed focus at a time
    • Instead of reading a chapter and trying to pick up on themes of social class and character psyches and story themes all at once, you should do these one at a time
  • Doing this led me towards being able to enjoy books I read in my own time better

Figure 1: https://www.amazon.ca/Game-Thrones-Song-Fire-Book/dp/0553573403

  • I distinctly remember once at work in October I was beginning to read the first book in A Song of Ice and Fire
    • Instead of spending so long in each chapter dissecting the bigger picture, with these literary lenses, it feels like I’m able to appreciate the smaller pieces more
  • In the first chapter I’m able to use Psychoanalytic Lens to think about the dynamics these unknown members of the Night’s Watch have together in their final moments
    • I’m able to think about how instinctual the Night’s Watch members are in general; they are an entire class of people who almost solely listen to their Id
  • I’m able to think about Daenerys’ chapters through Feminist Lens and ponder how the book is written as though women being married as children is normalized in this world
    • This is a writing choice I really appreciate by the way: internally when you read, you know it’s horrible, but it gives you a sense of the atmosphere of Essos
  • This unit, to conclude, really opened my eyes to how there is genuine meaning in the saying that “Everyone interprets the same media differently”
  • I have found myself more intrigued with, and making more use of some lenses in particular:

Marxist Lens

Figure 2: https://www.gettyimages.ca/photos/karl-marx

  • The issue of social and economic classes is the focus or a theme in many stories I enjoy, and as such, Marxist Lens appeals to me
  • It was very curious to see how even in a story as basic as The Three Little Pigs, if you analyze it from the right perspective, it is a commentary on capitalism
  • I just find Marxist Lens to be rather fun: many such reflections on civilization follow the exact same template of the Proletariat rising up against the Bourgeoisie

Figure 3: https://www.imdb.com/title/tt19850008/

  • One distinct example I can think of where I used Marxist Lens in recent memory is when I was re-watching The Batman
  • Previously I focused more on the Psyches of each character, but this time what caught my eye about the story was the class inequalities that motivated The Riddler
    • The Wayne family has always been the Bourgeoisie family of Gotham and in a way they, as well as the corrupt Government, have always interpellated the people
    • The common peoples of Gotham never even consider revolting against the corruption they live under until they’re given a voice by which to do so
  • The Riddler acts as a voice for the Proletariat against the scandals and wrongdoings of the Wayne’s in this movie
  • Bella Real acts as the democratic and more tame voice against the overall corruption that plagues Gotham and makes the city unsafe

Figure 4: https://uk.pinterest.com/pin/465278205243946845/

  • Another rather more obvious example of a piece of media just begging to be analyzed by the Marxist Lens is Mr. Robot
  • Here, the Bourgeoisie mega-corporations who manage the Base and manipulate the Superstructure of society are all represented by E-Corp, or Evil-Corp
  • The Crisis Point in this show happens at the end of the first season, a day referred to as 5/9
    • On this day, the protagonist group of hackers FSociety successfully erase all records of every form of debt that exist in the Evil-Corp banking system
  • Those who work for Evil-Corp are shown to be Exploited, and in specific, Angela is shown to suffer Alienation
    • She is discriminated against for being a woman which leads her on a hellbent mission which ultimately destroys her to break the glass ceiling she experiences

Psychonalytic Lenses

Figure 5: https://www.gettyimages.ca/photos/sigmund-freud

  • Thinking about the motivations and states of mind that characters display has always been one of my favourite ways to inspect media
  • I was surprised to find that I may be a bit of a drama person with the way I was able to fit my voice towards Edgar Allan Poe’s Tell-Tale Heart
  • The greatest appeal of many stories is the unique behaviour their characters have, and what I like in good stories is that even the most evil people have motivations

Figure 6: https://www.hbo.com/game-of-thrones/cast-and-crew/ramsay-snow

  • One of my favourite characters in a piece of fiction as of now most certainly has to be Ramsay Bolton as he is portrayed in Game of Thrones
  • Ramsay is very unique for he exhibits none of the empathy nor restraint people of the real world have; he is the embodiment of the Id taking control
  • His motivations are curious to me - he was born when the lord of the Dreadfort executed a miller for marrying without his permission, and forced himself upon his wife
    • When a child was born of this less than a year later, that lord saw grey eyes in the child, much like he himself had, and decided to raise him as his bastard
  • Ramsay took on the same ruthlessness as his father, and in the books it’s said that this corruption may have been due to his assigned servant which Ramsay called Reek
  • Being a bastard, Ramsay always tried to find approval in his father; he was raised to repress and deny the harm his cruelty caused
  • Ramsay quickly grew accustomed to displacing the pain he felt for being a bastard upon his victims of torture, murder, and worse; his unstable sense of self drove him

Figure 7: https://myrleynoella.pages.dev/odmsoyw-the-penguin-cast-2024-photos-dcenckx/

  • Another example of a character worth analyzing that really struck me in recent time was Oswald Cobblepot in his show The Penguin
  • Here, Oz begins the show presenting himself as a character who supposedly does not hide what he is, but he most certainly keeps his cards close to his chest
  • Beneath the mask he puts on is his shadow, hiding the pure rage he sometimes lets loose whenever he is belittled or treated as lesser, especially relating to his limp
  • Even further beyond that is his Anima, his nurturing side, especially relating to his mother, whom he exhibits an Oedipal Fixation on since his childhood
  • I won’t go too further into detail here since this show is still very new and very worth watching with a fresh set of eyes, but I absolutely loved this show

Unit 2: A Warning About the American Dream

Figure 8: https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1343092/mediaindex/

  • For me, reading The Great Gatsby was absolutely the best part of this course; it was the most fun and the most reflective portion
    • I had a blast listening to the audiobook for every chapter at home, and especially after the third chapter or so, I felt really engaged with the mystery of James Gatz
  • What the most enlightening part of this experience was, however, was to come to school the next day and discuss the various themes and character developments
  • It was a delight to hear the many interpretations of the same reading that can exist among 30 or so people
    • An example of this that I found particularly interesting was my interpretation of the billboard of Dr. T.J. Eckleberg contrasted with the interpretation others had
    • The sign was referred to as dilapidated and in the middle of the Valley of Ashes, so I thought this represented the ignorance the Bourgeoisie had for the Proletariat
    • I thought that perhaps this doctor was someone we could view as having moved onto a more wealthy lifestyle and has forgotten about his practice in the city
    • Of course, in class, what was expressed as the more common interpretation was that the eyes which the billboard illustrated represented those of God watching down
  • The common interpretation makes much more sense, and even fits more with one of the cover arts of the book, but what I like is that it doesn’t invalidate my thinking:

Figure 9: https://www.simonandschuster.co.uk/books/The-Great-Gatsby/F-Scott-Fitzgerald/9781471173936

  • Among our discussions, what continued to stand out to me above all was the theme of the American Dream
    • At first, I thought it was supposed to a theme represented exclusively by Tom, but as we got to know Gatsby better, I realized that was not at all the case
  • The more we read about Gatsby, and the more we approached the end of his story, the more I felt that I almost sympathized with him
  • In the end, this is what led me towards writing my unit essay arguing that he is an example of the Romantic Hero archetype:
  • I felt a relation to Gatsby in this way; he’s a person who has dedicated essentially their entire life to the pursuit of this perfect American Dream
    • Ultimately, he fails to see it through and dies before he’s able to have the life with Daisy that he’s been chasing for years
  • What this felt like to me was a warning; a warning to not give into obsessive goal-chasing, always trying to get the next best thing, otherwise it’ll consume me
  • This is something that I’m prone to, admittedly: my grandest vision of what a pristine life would be is going around the world, teaching Math at various Universities
    • I truly want to discover some new theorem or unknown fact in Math; it’s something I’ve fallen deeply in love with
  • I suppose what this book has taught me in the end is that my life isn’t purely defined by that goal, though; I can and should be willing to pivot if need be

Unit 3: The Technique of Making My Point

Figure 10: https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/37793.A_Doll_s_House

  • The Doll’s House continued this theme of the American Dream for me, but more critically than that, this unit taught me more about the art of debate
    • I’ve never done a debate as proper as I did for this unit, and something I really enjoyed was incorporating elements of ethics into the discussion
  • What was fun about this play was of course doing the voices for the characters, but I felt as though Parla did a particularly good job voicing Krogstad
    • She generally got me incredibly engaged with the character
    • I saw Krogstad as a sleazy shrewd businessman already, but Parla’s tone brought out the malice in his actions a lot more
  • Beyond that though, I also saw the sympathy that could be felt for Krogstad
    • All people are inherently selfish, and all people make mistakes, and it didn’t feel fair that Krogstad should be fired for a single misstep
  • That’s exactly what made debating the ethics of Krogstad as a character very interesting in the end
  • At first, thanks to Alex’s misunderstanding which I totally took no part or responsibility in, we prepared to defend Krogstad’s character
  • We were prepared to argue the case of the Utilitarian Consequentialist
    • Krogstad’s actions in total had a good outcome since they revealed to Nora that she was unhappy in her current relationship
    • Thus, Krogstad’s actions were in the end, morally justifiable
  • Once the tables were turned on us, however, we argued the case of the Deontologist
    • Krogstad lets his Id take control by asserting power over Nora even though she has done nothing wrong
    • This is an action that is simply wrong for the sake of it not being the right thing to do
    • It is never right to blackmail someone under the Deontologist viewpoint
